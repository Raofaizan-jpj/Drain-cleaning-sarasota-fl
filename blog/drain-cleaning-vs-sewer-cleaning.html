Drain Cleaning vs. Sewer Cleaning: What Is the Difference?

While often used interchangeably, drain cleaning and sewer cleaning address completely different parts of your home's plumbing infrastructure.

Home » Blog » Drain Cleaning vs. Sewer Cleaning: Key Differences Explained

Homeowners often hear plumbing professionals mention 'drain cleaning' and 'sewer cleaning.' Although both services restore drainage flow, they involve different pipe diameters, distinct equipment, and separate parts of your home's wastewater system.

Direct Answer: Drain Cleaning vs. Sewer Cleaning

Quick Summary: Drain cleaning addresses small interior branch pipes (1.5 to 2 inches) connected to individual sinks, tubs, and showers. Sewer cleaning focuses on the main lateral line (3 to 6 inches) that collects waste from the entire house and delivers it to the municipal sewer main.

Key Differences Side by Side

  • Pipe Diameter: Drain lines measure 1.5 to 2 inches; sewer lines measure 3, 4, or 6 inches in diameter.
  • Access Points: Drain cleaning is performed through interior P-traps, overflow drains, or small cleanouts. Sewer cleaning is performed through large exterior sewer cleanouts or roof vent stacks.
  • Typical Debris: Drains clog from hair, soap scum, and food particles. Sewer lines clog from tree roots, thick grease accumulations, flushable wipes, and ground sediment.
  • Equipment: Drains are cleared with hand snakes or small electric sink augers. Sewer lines require high-torque commercial cabling machines, heavy root cutters, or high-volume hydro jetting rigs.

How to Tell Which Service You Need

If only your kitchen sink is backed up, you need localized drain cleaning. If multiple fixtures are gurgling, water is rising in ground-floor showers when you flush the toilet, or wastewater is backing up across the entire house, you need main sewer cleaning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sewer cleaning more expensive than drain cleaning?

Sewer cleaning typically involves larger commercial equipment, heavier cabling, or hydro jetting rigs, making it slightly more comprehensive than clearing a simple bathroom sink trap.

How do technicians access the main sewer line?

Technicians access the main sewer through an exterior ground-level cleanout pipe located in your yard, near the foundation, or through a vertical roof vent.
